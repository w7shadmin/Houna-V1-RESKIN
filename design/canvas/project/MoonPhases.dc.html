Houna moon — real phases

The starfield’s moon as tonight’s real moon: its phase worked out from the date alone, the way weather apps do it. No weather app, no internet, no permissions. On new-moon nights the dark part stays as a faint disc with the logo just visible (earthshine), so the scene is never empty.

A · The glow breathes The moon keeps tonight’s exact shape; its halo swells on the in-breath and ebbs on the out-breath, as the moon does today.
B · The light breathes too Around tonight’s shape, the lit part swells a little on the in-breath and eases back on the out. A thin sliver appears even at new moon; a full moon can’t grow, so only its halo breathes.

How it knowsThe moon repeats every 29.53 days, counted from a known new moon. From the phone’s date and time, that gives tonight’s phase to within a few hours: the same figures a weather app shows, with nothing to fetch and nothing to allow.
Why not the weather appOther apps’ data isn’t readable: Android weather apps don’t share it, and Apple’s WeatherKit needs a paid developer setup and an internet call, for numbers we can work out for free.
As seen from the GulfThe lit side is drawn as it looks from the northern hemisphere: on the right as it grows, on the left as it shrinks. Later, it could mark the Hijri month too: the first thin crescent is the month’s start.
